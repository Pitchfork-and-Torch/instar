What Cicada 3301 actually was

Starting 4 January 2012, an anonymous account posted a JPEG to 4chan. The image hid a Caesar-family string, then a Vigenere keyed by the emperor Tiberius Claudius Caesar, then OutGuess steganography, then a book cipher, then an audio spectrogram, then physical posters with QR codes in several cities, then a Tor hidden service. PGP signatures signed the statements. 3301 is a prime.

2013 repeated the pattern with more onions. 2014 published Liber Primus, a rune book whose remaining pages are still unsolved in public. The group said it sought highly intelligent individuals. It never proved a government affiliation. It never needed to. The work itself selected for patience, tool fluency, and the refusal to stop at the first obvious layer.

INSTAR is not that group. We do not collect identities. We do not run a hidden service that pretends to hire you. We rebuilt the moves as a school so the next person who meets a doctored JPEG already has the habits: view source, invert the alphabet, fetch the book, extract the least bit, look at the spectrogram, factor the toy modulus, and treat unsolved runes as unsolved.

If you want the real primary sources, read the signed PGP messages archived by the solvers, and the Liber Primus transcriptions. Do not harass people who worked the 2012 posters. Do not claim a solution you cannot show.
