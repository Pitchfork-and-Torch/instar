Instar V · Prime

Two primes, then a door.

Cicada used large primes and RSA as a handshake: anyone can encrypt to a public n and e; only the holder of d can read. Here the primes are classroom-sized so you can factor them by hand or with the workbench. The plaintext letters are ASCII code points.

phi = (p-1)(q-1). d is the inverse of e mod phi. m = c^d mod n.

Tradecraft

Real RSA is 2048+ bits. Factoring this n is a lesson, not a break. Cicada also asked people to multiply large primes as proof of work. 3301 itself is prime. That was a calling card, not a cipher.

The RSA bench will factor, invert, and decrypt if you do not want to do the arithmetic in a notebook. It also forges a classroom pair so you can see n and d born.
